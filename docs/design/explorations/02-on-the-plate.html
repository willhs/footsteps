    What it shows

    On the map: quiet grey outlines with a plain label for the main food of each region: "wheat & barley", "rice", "millet", "maize", "taro & yam", "milk & meat", "fish & sea mammals". Grown areas are hatched; wild ones are dotted. Scrub time and they grow out of their homelands (wheat and barley from the Fertile Crescent after ~9000 BCE, rice from the Yangtze, millet from the Yellow River, maize from Mexico). Before 10,000 BCE everything is "wild plants & game".

    In the details panel: a plate split by rough share of calories: wild plants, crops, hunted meat, herd meat, milk, fish. Hatched slices are wild. Below it: named foods, the staple as recorded, how much land around is cropland, the lean season, and "Imagine a meal".

    Over time: a stacked band on the slider: the world's food from the wild, from crops and from herds. Wild is ~100% until ~10,000 BCE and a few percent by 1900.

    Kind

    • Measured: cropland and grazing share of the land around (HYDE 3.5, from 10,000 BCE). Sourced: the staple (D-PLACE EA029) and subsistence shares (EA001–EA005) of the nearest recorded society, named with its record year.
    • Modelled: the slices on the plate (subsistence shares × staple × way of life), the staple outlines on the map, and the world band.
    • Imagined: a meal: who cooks, what's in the pot, who eats first. Grounded in the rows above it.

    Verdict: have it for the staple and the shares; can get it for dish-level detail. The atlas already has the staple and subsistence shares per cell, and HYDE has the land. The crop-spread outlines are a small hand-made table. Dishes are best left to the imagined meal, fed by the facts.

    Risks

    • Recent records, ancient eras. The atlas describes societies seen ~1850–1950. A 1950 Kalahari camp is not 5000 BCE. Always name the society and year.
    • Hard edges. Outlines can read like borders. Keep them faint, soft and labelled "main food", never filled with colour.
    • Calories hide nutrition. Meat and fish matter more than their calorie share. Say "rough share of calories".
    • Famine as drama. Famines are well recorded in states and invisible elsewhere. Keep it to one lean-season line, not a famine map.

    Gender and war

    Gender: grinding grain, cooking and serving are often split by sex in ethnographies, but the split varies a lot. Leave it off by default. Show "who cooks" only from a sourced record (D-PLACE EA044–EA054 sex differences in tasks), naming the society, never as a general rule.

    War: leave sieges and war famines out. A failed harvest is shown as a lean-season note, not a cause. Concept 05 handles conflict.

    Alternatives considered

    ShapeWhy not (or why)
    Plate in the panel + staple outlines on the map (shown)The plate is instantly readable and compares well between places; the outlines show the big story (crops spreading) without colour.
    A "food web" radiating from the clicked place: lines to the river, the herds, the fields, the forest, sized by shareShows where food came from in space, which the plate can't. Busier, and the distances would be invented. Worth a later look for foragers.
    Colour-coded staple map (rice green, wheat gold...)Rejected: breaks the one-warm-colour rule, implies hard borders, and turns the map into a textbook plate.
